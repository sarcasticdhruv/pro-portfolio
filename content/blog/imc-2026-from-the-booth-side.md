I've watched a lot of tech events through livestreams and LinkedIn carousels. IMC 2026 was the first one I got to see from the other side of the table, wearing a badge that said AI LifeBOT, explaining our product to whoever walked up.

Short version: it was loud, it was huge, my feet hurt, and I'd do it again tomorrow.

## So what is IMC, quickly

India Mobile Congress is the country's biggest telecom and tech event, organised by the Department of Telecommunications and the Cellular Operators Association of India. This year was the 10th edition, held at Yashobhoomi in New Delhi from October 7 to 10, under the theme "Scale Without Boundaries." Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated it on October 8.

The scale is a little hard to picture until you're inside it. According to [Communications Today's wrap-up](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/imc-2026-closes-with-indias-bid-to-own-its-technology/), the event drew around 150,000 attendees from more than 100 countries, 600+ partners and exhibitors, 500+ startups, 900+ speakers across 120+ sessions, and more than 2,000 technology use cases on display. For context, the first IMC in 2017 had about 32,000 visitors.

The official focus areas were 6G, AI-native networks, satellite connectivity, cybersecurity and digital infrastructure, as [India TV covered on day one](https://www.indiatvnews.com/technology/news/india-mobile-congress-2026-focusing-majorly-on-6g-ai-satellite-communication-cybersecurity-and-more-2026-10-07-1056333). In practice, AI showed up in almost every booth no matter what the sign said.

## The big booths

When I could step away, I walked the floor. A few things stood out.

**Ericsson** had the most complete story about AI inside the network. Their [showcase](https://telecomtalk.info/imc2026-ericsson-showcases-5g-ai-6g-innovations/1012599/) covered AI-driven radio performance and energy efficiency, agentic AI for automating network operations, AI smart glasses doing live translation over 5G, and AI-native collaborative robots running in a mock warehouse. They also featured six startups from their Innovation Challenge, including KissanAI, which does voice and vision AI for farmers. That one stuck with me because I've built in that space too, with [AI Kisaan Sahayak](/blog/ai-kisaan-sahayak).

**Jio** showed hardware that supports both 5G and 6G, including a Giga MIMO radio, and talked about AI use cases for farmers, doctors and students ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/technology/jio-at-imc-2026-india-could-be-among-the-first-nations-to-deploy-6g-4176935)).

**Qualcomm** framed its booth around "connectivity plus compute," with on-device AI agents and a live integrated sensing demo with Tejas Networks ([ANI](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/qualcomm-highlights-connectivity-plus-compute-at-imc-2026-as-agentic-ai-reshapes-network-architecture20261009154837/)).

**Airtel** was less about flashy demos and more about the plumbing: sovereign cloud, network intelligence and AI-based security operations.

## The idea everyone kept coming back to

If I had to sum up IMC 2026 in one line: AI is moving from something that runs *on* the network to something that runs *inside* it.

Telecom companies talked about AI agents that watch network traffic, find faults, and fix them without waiting for a human. The zero-touch network sessions dealt with the harder part of that: closed-loop automation, and guardrails for AI agents acting on live infrastructure. A recurring line in the security discussions was that high-impact AI decisions still need human approval.

That landed for me. I've written about [what happens when agents act outside their sandbox](/blog/the-agents-broke-containment), and I built [Helix](/blog/why-i-built-helix) mostly because agents in production need hard limits, not good intentions. Hearing operators who run networks for hundreds of millions of people reach the same conclusion felt like a good sign. The industry is excited about agentic AI, but the serious people are spending at least as much time on the brakes as on the engine.

The other theme was ownership. The government talked about targeting 10% of global 6G patents through the Bharat 6G Alliance, and the IndiaAI Mission has onboarded more than 38,000 GPUs, with about 20,000 more being added. India's message was clear: we want to build this technology, not just use it. The honest coverage also pointed out the gaps, like dependence on imported semiconductors and components, and that some of this is still a roadmap rather than results.

## The part that wasn't about the tech

My biggest takeaway had very little to do with any demo.

The value was in the conversations: people from completely different corners of the industry describing problems we'd never have thought to solve, use cases that only come up when someone from a logistics company and someone from a bank are standing at the same booth, and the "wait, could your thing do X?" moments that turn into real collaborations later. You can read every launch announcement from home. You can't get those conversations from home.

## FAQ

**What is India Mobile Congress (IMC)?**

India Mobile Congress is India's largest telecom and technology event, organised by the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) and the Cellular Operators Association of India (COAI). The 2026 edition was the 10th, held at Yashobhoomi, New Delhi, from October 7 to 10, under the theme "Scale Without Boundaries."

**What were the main themes at IMC 2026?**

6G, AI-native networks, satellite connectivity, cybersecurity and digital infrastructure. In practice, AI ran through nearly everything, from agentic AI automating network operations to on-device AI agents and AI-based security.

**What did AI LifeBOT showcase at IMC 2026?**

Pitch Twin, an AI Sales Coach, along with AI LifeBOT's work in voice-first AI, conversational AI agents, agentic AI, and enterprise process automation.

**Is IMC worth attending for an AI engineer, not just telecom people?**

Yes. A lot of the most interesting work there is AI work: agents running live infrastructure, guardrails for automated decisions, and real deployment problems at a scale most AI demos never touch. The conversations with people outside your usual bubble are worth the trip on their own.
